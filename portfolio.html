Meta Quest 3 Teachers Guide

September 2026–Present

VIEW COMPLETE PROJECT ↗VIEW SCREENSHOT ↗

Introduction

I began developing the Meta Quest 3 Teachers Guide in September 2026 and continue to refine it. This web-based resource introduces teachers and other first-time users to the headset, its controls, basic navigation, and learning activities. It combines visual guidance with practical setup instructions to make an unfamiliar device easier to explore. The intended audience is educators preparing to use virtual reality in teaching. Audience feedback has not yet been documented in this portfolio.

Conclusion & Significance

This artifact shows how I translate unfamiliar technology into a manageable learning experience. The guide connects visual explanations with practical tasks so teachers can move from identifying controls to using the headset. It reflects my focus on clear instructions, purposeful technology use, and helping learners build confidence before entering a new environment.

Introduction

Color-coded service cards and consistent navigation organize these options into a clear starting point. The intended audience is people seeking information and support for international education. The project connects my background in international education with my interest in designing useful, approachable tools for students and families.

Teotihuacan VR Museum

September 2026–Present

EDUC 767 · Group project with Ava and Regan

VIEW COMPLETE PROJECT ↗VIEW SCREENSHOT ↗

Introduction

I began developing Teotihuacan VR Museum with Ava and Regan in September 2026 as a group assignment for EDUC 767. Built in Frame, this virtual museum explores how an immersive environment can make abstract knowledge more concrete and memorable. Our aim was to experiment with spatial layout, directional sound, controller vibration or interaction, and stereoscopic vision as possible supports for learning and memory. The intended audience is learners exploring Teotihuacan through a virtual museum experience. The project remains in development, and audience feedback has not yet been documented in this portfolio.

Conclusion & Significance

This project helped me think about learning design beyond a screen or a page. Working with Ava and Regan gave me an opportunity to connect educational ideas with the design of a shared virtual space. Its significance lies in exploring how sensory cues and purposeful interaction might support understanding and recall. These are design intentions rather than verified learning outcomes.

UNC School of Social Work Canvas Design

August 2026–Present

First internship project · Canvas & DesignPLUS · Ten courses

VIEW CANVAS COURSE ↗VIEW SCREENSHOT ↗

Course access may require UNC login and enrollment. A screenshot is available above.

Introduction

Since August 2026, my first internship project with the UNC School of Social Work has involved designing and adjusting content across ten Canvas courses. I began with Digital Accessibility Awareness and Digital Accessibility in Course Design to build a foundation for the work. I then learned to use Canvas and DesignPLUS to create and adapt banner titles, icon grids, content blocks, and course templates with accessibility requirements in mind. The intended audience is the students and instructors using these courses. The screenshot shows a SOWO 330 course page. Audience feedback has not yet been documented in this portfolio.

Conclusion & Significance

This project connects my teaching background with practical instructional design in higher education. It shows my progression from learning about digital accessibility to applying that knowledge in a learning management system. Working across ten courses has given me opportunities to consider consistent navigation, reusable layouts, and clear content organization. The experience is shaping my approach to designing course materials around learner access and usability.

China’s Double Reduction Policy

March 1, 2022

Arizona State University · GLE 503 · Final paper

READ COMPLETE PAPER ↗DOWNLOAD PDF ↓

Introduction

I completed this paper on March 1, 2022, for GLE 503: Evaluating Global Education Policies and Programs at Arizona State University. Written during the COVID-19 pandemic, it examines China's Double Reduction policy, introduced in July 2021, and its implications for international education. Using the school where I worked as a case, I discuss homework management, school-based English support, teacher development, and enrichment activities for students in Grades 7–9. The paper includes a logic model, an implementation plan, and a mixed-method evaluation approach. Its intended audience was the course instructor and others interested in education policy and program evaluation. Instructor feedback has not yet been documented in this portfolio.

Conclusion & Significance

This artifact shows how I connect education policy with decisions about teaching, learner support, and program evaluation. It helped me examine the tension between reducing family burdens and increasing schools' responsibilities during a period of disruption. It also strengthened my attention to equity, teacher workload, and the difference between intended outcomes and evidence of impact. The paper offers a school-specific perspective rather than a national assessment of the policy.
