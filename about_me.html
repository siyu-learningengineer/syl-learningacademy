About Me

Professional Background

I have over ten years of experience in international education that includes teaching, academic leadership, curriculum development, and management of educational programs. In my positions as Director of College Counseling, Principal’s Assistant, Director of Studies, and language teacher, I developed learner-focused courses and materials, provided professional development for teachers, assessed their teaching, and managed educational programs. I have also employed such educational technologies as online learning systems, digital media, and interactive exercises to make teaching easier, interesting, and learner-friendly. Currently, by studying at UNC-Chapel Hill and working in an internship with the UNC School of Social Work, I am gaining knowledge and experience in course development on Canvas, digital accessibility, generative AI, and technology-assisted learning.

Career Aspirations & Goals

The objective of my career is to join the profession of being a learning experience designer who brings together education, technology, and learning requirements into practice. My goal is to create accessible, interactive, and learner-focused educational experiences at different institutions, including schools, universities, and various other organizations. In the long run, I wish to lead some learning programs and use AI ethically to make quality education personalized and inclusive.

Personal Interests

The exploration of how technology and artificial intelligence could enhance education by making it more reflective and personalized is something that interests me immensely. The emphasis of my work lies in helping students from Grade 9 through 12 develop a greater awareness of their abilities, analyze their preparedness for college, and make future decisions. One of my passions includes investigating novel approaches to learning technology and coming up with creative ways to make sure that the learning process aligns with students' needs and interests. It is a simple concept – technology is not supposed to replace teachers; instead, it should help students become better learners.
